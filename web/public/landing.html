    Sample tokens.

    Three verdicts, always with the reason.

    Pools, bonding curves and protocol wallets are left out, so only real wallets count. Every verdict names the one fact that decided it.

    Bundled

    Stay out.

    The launch was taken by one player, or the dev has done this many times before.

    • 30% or more of the supply bought in the first 10 seconds
    • One wallet holding 20% or more
    • Wallets funded by the same source holding a large share
    • Bundlers or insiders holding 20% or more
    • A serial dev (20+ launches) or a dev farm
    Watch

    Look twice.

    Nothing proves a rug, but something isn't right.

    • 15% or more taken in the first seconds
    • Mint or freeze authority still active
    • Top 10 wallets holding 40% or more
    • Liquidity under 5% of market cap
    • A dev who sold most of the launch buy, or 5+ past launches
    Clean

    Nothing found.

    None of the warning signs. A fact about the launch, not a promise.

    • A spread-out launch, no early grab
    • No single wallet in control
    • No funding links between the top holders
    • A dev with no rug history
    • A clean token can still go to zero

    Read-only. And you can check it.

    An extension sees the pages you visit, so you should know exactly what this one does. Here it is, in plain words.

    What Vigil does

    • Reads token addresses on the sites listed above, nowhere else.
    • Sends those addresses to Vigil's server, which reads the chain and answers with a verdict.
    • Remembers one setting on your computer: which server to ask.

    What Vigil never does

    • Never touches a wallet. No connection, no signature, no access to funds.
    • Never reads your history, your cookies or any other site.
    • Never asks for an account, an email or a payment.

    The code is public

    The whole extension is a few small files you can read before installing. The only permission it asks the browser for is storage, plus access to the sites above.

    Questions

    Facts, not financial advice. Vigil shows what happened on-chain; the decision stays yours.

    What is Vigil?

    A free browser extension that detects rugs on Solana memecoins. It doesn't trade and it doesn't replace your terminal: it sits on top of the sites you already use and puts a Clean, Watch or Bundled verdict next to every token.

    Which sites does it work on?

    Axiom, GMGN, pump.fun, DexScreener and X today. Padre, Photon and BullX are in beta: badges show up on their token pages and links.

    How fast is a verdict?

    A few seconds for a token nobody has checked yet, instantly after that: verdicts are shared, so the next trader who sees the token gets it straight away.

    Does it see my wallet or my funds?

    No. Vigil never asks to connect a wallet and never signs anything. The only thing it sends is the token addresses shown on your screen.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. No subscription, no account, nothing to pay.

    Why isn't it on the Chrome Web Store yet?

    The listing is in review. Until it's live, you install the same extension by hand in four steps. Once it's on the store, it will install in one click and update itself.

    Is a Clean verdict a buy signal?

    No. A verdict is a fact about how the token launched and who holds it, not a prediction. A clean token can still go to zero.

    Can I check a token without the extension?

    Yes: paste a contract address in the web scanner. It runs the same scan, with the chart, the holders map and the exit radar.

    What is $VIGIL?

    The community token behind the project. You don't need it to use the extension.
